What this is

A short introduction to the DHDE research line, the published paper, and the live demos on this site.

DHDE (Distributed Human Data Engine) is a tourism-flow research effort out of the University of Fukui's Headquarters for Regional Revitalization, developed under the Sakura Science Program. It combines digital-intent signals, behavioral survey data, and ground-truth sensing to model and forecast visitor flow at tourism and heritage sites, with the aim of smoothing congestion and surfacing under-visited places rather than just measuring foot traffic after the fact.

The method was first applied to Fukui and the wider Hokuriku region in Japan, and is the subject of a peer-reviewed paper. It is now being explored, as a concept-stage prototype, for the heritage and museum sites of Madinah, Saudi Arabia, in collaboration with Taibah University's College of Engineering.

The two demos linked from this page are at different stages: the Fukui demo reflects the published research; the Madinah demo is an early concept prototype built to illustrate how the same method could apply there, with illustrative placeholder data pending real figures from Madinah management.
